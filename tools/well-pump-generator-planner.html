It answers

“What size generator do I need to run my well pump, furnace and fridge at the same time?” and “In what order should I turn things on so a smaller generator copes?”

You get

  • Running total of everything checked
  • Peak if you stagger starts vs. peak if everything starts at once
  • A running rating (÷0.8) and a starting rating to look for
  • A 240 V warning when a well pump is selected
  • A start order, biggest surge first, ~10 s apart

How the math works

running total = Σ running W of checked loads largest surge = max(starting W − running W) staggered peak = running total + largest surge worst case = Σ starting W recommended = running ≥ running total ÷ 0.8 (rounded up to 100 W) starting ≥ staggered peak (rounded up to 100 W)

Worked example

With the planner’s default loads (½ HP pump 1,000/2,100 W, fridge 700/2,200 W, gas furnace blower 800/2,350 W, 10 LED lights 100 W, Wi-Fi 25 W; all planning figures estimate):

running total 2,625 W · staggered peak 4,175 W · all-at-once 6,775 W → look for ≈ 3,300 W running and ≈ 4,200 W starting

Quick check: nameplate to watts

Without JavaScript: running watts ≈ volts × amps. A motor's starting watts are often 2–3× running (estimate).

Limits to know

  • Default watts are typical planning figures, not your equipment. Replace them with nameplate numbers.
  • A well pump’s starting draw is often 2–3× running; motors with a soft start surge much less.
  • The planner doesn’t design the house connection. A transfer switch or interlock must be chosen and installed by a licensed electrician. Never backfeed through a dryer outlet.
