Well Pressure Tank Sizer

Pick a bladder pressure tank whose published drawdown covers at least a minute of your pump’s flow, so the pump stops short-cycling. Also tells you the precharge and flags an undersized existing tank.

Free, no sign-up, runs in your browser at well-pressure-tank-sizer.vercel.app.

It answers

“What size pressure tank do I need for my pump and switch setting?” and “Is my current tank too small?”

You get

  • Required drawdown in gallons
  • The smallest tank on the Amtrol Well-X-Trol reference table that meets it, with model
  • Precharge setting (cut-in − 2 psi)
  • A warning if your current tank looks undersized
  • The full drawdown reference table with your pick highlighted

What to have ready (and where to find it)

InputWhere to find it
Pump flow (GPM)Well driller’s log, installer invoice, or the pump model’s spec sheet. Nameplate GPM wins over guesses.
Minimum run time1 minute is the common target; 2 minutes is safer on larger pumps. Use the pump maker’s figure if given.
Pressure switch settingWatch the gauge through one cycle: low point is cut-in, high point is cut-out (e.g. 30/50, 40/60).
Current tank size (optional)Label on the tank.

How the math works

drawdown needed = GPM × minimum run minutes pick = smallest tank whose published drawdown at your switch setting ≥ drawdown needed precharge = cut-in − 2 psi (tank empty) 20/40 settings = estimated (~15% above 30/50; not on the card)

Worked example

10 GPM × 1 min = 10 gal needed at 30/50 WX-203 (32 gal) ≈ 9.9 gal → short WX-205 (34 gal) ≈ 10.5 gal → pick · precharge 28 psi

Drawdowns from Amtrol Well-X-Trol specifications card MC7025 as published in the sizer.

Quick check: minimum drawdown and precharge

Without JavaScript: drawdown needed = GPM × minutes. Precharge = cut-in − 2 psi, set with the tank empty.

Limits to know

  • Drawdowns come from one brand’s published card. Other brands differ; check the chart for the tank you buy.
  • A waterlogged tank acts small whatever its label. Check precharge before replacing.
  • Constant-pressure (variable-speed) systems follow their own tank and precharge rules.
