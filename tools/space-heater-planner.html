It answers

“Will this space heater trip my breaker?”, “Is it cheaper than my central heat?” and “Does heating one room and turning the thermostat down actually save money?”

You get

  • Amps and watts on the circuit against the breaker and its 80% line, with a plain verdict
  • Cost per hour and per million BTU for a heater vs. each fuel
  • A net monthly estimate for zone heating with a thermostat setback
  • CPSC safety basics and marked gear links

What to have ready (and where to find it)

Heater settingsHeater label or manual: commonly 1,500 W high with lower 600–1,000 W modes.

How the math works

amps = watts ÷ 120 V 80% line = 12 A (1,440 W) on 15 A · 16 A (1,920 W) on 20 A $/MMBtu fuel = price ÷ heat content × 1,000,000 ÷ efficiency $/MMBtu heater = $/kWh ÷ 3,412 × 1,000,000 (÷ COP for a heat pump) setback saving ≈ 1% of heating cost per °F per 8 h, capped at 10°F (DOE-based estimate)

Worked example

1,500 W heater alone on 15 A = 12.5 A → just over the 12 A line + TV 100 W + laptop 65 W = 13.9 A → over the guideline Two 1,500 W heaters = 25 A → trips

Run your own prices in the planner for the cost side; we don’t repeat EIA prices here because they change weekly.

Quick check: will this circuit hold?

Without JavaScript: amps = watts ÷ volts. Keep continuous loads at or under 12 A (1,440 W) on 15 A, 16 A (1,920 W) on 20 A.

Limits to know

  • Prices are EIA averages for the dates the planner shows. Your bill is more accurate.
  • Typical device watts are estimates. A plug-in watt meter gives real numbers.
  • The setback savings rule is an estimate; insulation and climate change the result.
