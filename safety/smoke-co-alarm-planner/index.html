Section 2 · Field guide

Where is the date on my alarm?

  • Turn the alarm over. Twist it off its mounting plate and look at the label on the back. Kidde says the manufacture date code is stamped on that label, near the model number. First Alert likewise says to look at the back of the alarm for the date of manufacture.
  • It's a manufacture date, not an expiration date. First Alert's FAQ notes the date printed on the back is the date the unit was made.
  • Count from manufacture, not from when you installed it. NFPA states smoke alarms must be replaced 10 years after their manufacturing date. An alarm that sat on a shelf for two years has two fewer years left.
  • If the label prints a "replace by" date, choose that option above and enter it as-is. That printed date beats any rule of thumb here.
  • No date, or can't read it? Kidde advises replacing the alarm if there's any doubt about its age. Log it as "No readable date" and it will be flagged.
