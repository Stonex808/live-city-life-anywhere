Math: fuel per hour = a no-load burn for the generator class + about 0.7 lb of propane per kWh you draw (0.6 for big portables), converted to gasoline at 5.6 lb of propane per gallon by energy. We fitted those planning numbers to published maker runtimes, and they come out a bit higher than most spec sheets on purpose. Total stock adds a 15% reserve. Your manual's runtime chart beats ours.

Before you fill up

  • Generator outside, 20 ft from doors and windows. Never in a garage, even with the door open. Check your alarms: CO alarm planner.
  • Run fewer hours, not a bigger generator. A fridge holds cold in 2–4 hour blocks: fridge food clock.
  • Pair it with a battery. Charge a power station while the generator runs, then shut it off overnight.
  • Gas goes stale. Champion says stabilized gasoline keeps up to a year in a cool, dry place. Pour stored gas into your car every few months and refill.
  • Cold kills small propane tanks. One 20-lb cylinder can only boil off so much gas: at 20°F, about 13,000 BTU an hour (US Carburetion chart). That's roughly 0.6 lb an hour. Use a 100-lb cylinder or two tanks on a manifold.
  • Stop the engine and let it cool before refueling. Store cans and cylinders outdoors or in a detached shed, upright, away from flames. Check your local limits on stored fuel.
