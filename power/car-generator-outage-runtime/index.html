Math: hours = gallons ÷ (idle burn + electric load burn). Idle burn with no load: 0.17 gal/h for a 2.0 L car (Argonne National Laboratory, via Oak Ridge's Fact of the Week #861); a 4.6 L sedan burns just over twice that. Our V6, V8 and hybrid rates are estimates. Electric load burn: every 1,000 W of average load adds about 0.2 gal/h (we assume ~15% of gas energy reaches your outlet at idle). Average load counts fridges, freezers, furnace blowers and sump pumps as running part of the time (our duty-cycle estimates). EV: hours = kWh × 0.9 ÷ load. Planning numbers, not tests of any vehicle.

Car exhaust kills. Read this first.

  • Never run the car in a garage, even with the door open. Park outside, tailpipe pointed away from the house, well away from doors, windows and vents.
  • Put a battery CO alarm inside near sleeping areas. Headache, dizziness or nausea: get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911.
  • Clear snow from the tailpipe before and during every run. A blocked pipe pushes exhaust into the cabin.
  • Never backfeed a wall outlet. A hardwired furnace needs an electrician-installed inlet or transfer switch.

Make it work

  • The 12 V plug tops out around 150 W. Phones, router, lights. A fridge needs a 1,000 W+ inverter clamped to the battery with the short, thick cables it ships with.
  • Buy pure sine wave for fridges, furnace blowers and CPAPs. Modified sine can make motors buzz and run hot.
  • Keep the engine running under any real load. An inverter can flatten a starting battery in under an hour.
  • Stagger starts. Let the fridge kick on before the furnace blower. Surges stack. We assume an inverter handles about 2× its rating for a few seconds; check yours.
  • Cycle it. A closed fridge holds about 4 hours. Run it a few hours, rest a few, and stretch the tank.
  • Better: charge a power station from the car a few hours a day, then run the house quietly from the station.
