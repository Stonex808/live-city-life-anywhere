Math: hours = usable battery Wh ÷ watts. Lead-acid UPS: we count about 60% of the battery's nominal Wh (12 V × Ah) as reaching your gear, after inverter loss and lead-acid losses. Power station: 85% of rated Wh minus about 10 W the inverter uses itself. "Battery for your hours" adds a 20% reserve for battery age. These percentages are our planning assumptions, not tests of any model.

Before you buy

  • Back up every box between the wall and your Wi-Fi. Modem or fiber box (ONT), router, and any mesh node you need. The fiber box is often in a garage or basement on its own outlet.
  • Your internet provider has to stay up too. If the neighborhood node loses power, your Wi-Fi works but goes nowhere. Keep phone hotspot data as plan B.
  • Small UPS = minutes for a PC, hours for a router. Use it to save and shut the PC down; keep the network running on what's left.
  • Want a power station as a UPS? Pick one that lists UPS or automatic switchover in milliseconds, so gear doesn't reboot when the grid drops.
  • Measure, don't guess. A $15–25 plug-in watt meter shows real draw. Adapter labels list the maximum, not the normal.
  • Router on DC? A 12 V mini DC UPS or a DC cable from a power station skips the inverter and runs longer.
