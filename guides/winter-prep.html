Winter prep for rural and backup-powered homes

A practical checklist for the season when pipes freeze, power fails and fuel trucks run late. Tick items as you go (they’re saved on this device) or print it and tape it inside a cupboard.

Do these three first

1) Find and label the main water shutoff and the well pump breaker. 2) Put CO alarms with batteries on every level. 3) Write your backup-power start order and tape it inside the panel door.

1. Pipes (before the first freeze)

Most freeze damage happens where pipes run through unheated space: crawlspaces, exterior walls, garages, attics and the line from the well.

2. Well house and pump

On a well, the pressure tank, pressure switch and the pipe from the well are your city water main. Keep them above freezing.

3. Backup heat

Know how you’ll stay warm if the power is out for two days, before it happens.

4. Fuel

Rural fuel runs on delivery schedules. Storms make them longer.

5. Backup power

The moment the lights go out is a bad time to discover the generator won’t start.

6. Outage kit

Assume no water from the tap, no heat from the furnace and no internet for a few days.

7. During a hard freeze

When the forecast drops well below freezing, do these the night before.

8. If a pipe freezes or bursts

On a well, cut the pump too

A burst pipe keeps flooding as long as the pump keeps running. Shut the main water valve and turn off the well pump breaker. If water is near electrical outlets or the panel, stay out of the water and call for help.

  1. Frozen, not burst: open the tap it feeds so melting water can flow out.
  2. Warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer, heating pad or warm towels, starting nearest the tap. Never use an open flame or torch.
  3. If you can’t find or reach the frozen section, or it’s in the well line, call a plumber or well contractor.
  4. After thawing, check along the pipe for leaks before you leave it.

Leaving the property for the winter?

Shut off and drain the water system, turn off the pump breaker, and have a neighbor check the house. A plumber can winterize traps and fixtures properly; that’s worth the call for a long absence.
